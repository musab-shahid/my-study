## 5.7.1 Components, anomalies, and missing data

### 5.7.1.1 Trend, seasonality, residual
An observed series can be thought of as slow change + repeating seasonal cycle + irregular residual. Classical additive decomposition and STL (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess) are practical starting points. **Season definitions for Pakistan work must be justified** (not default JJA only)—see Unit 2.8.

### 5.7.1.2 Anomalies
\[
a_t = x_t - \bar{x}_{\mathrm{season}(t),\,\mathrm{baseline}}
\]
Fix the baseline period **before** analysis (e.g. 1991–2020 WMO-style normal). Report baseline, season rule, and product in every trend or anomaly figure caption.

### 5.7.1.3 Missing data and gap filling
Seasonal missingness biases monthly means if you drop incomplete months silently. Interpolation and model-based fills encode assumptions—**flag filled points** in downstream products and sensitivity-test key results with/without fills.

## 5.7.2 Autocorrelation and stationarity

### 5.7.2.1 ACF and PACF
The lag-\(k\) autocorrelation measures linear dependence between \(x_t\) and \(x_{t-k}\). Climate series often show strong lag-1 memory. Use ACF/PACF to: (1) justify effective sample size adjustments; (2) guide ARIMA orders; (3) check residuals after trend removal.

### 5.7.2.2 Stationarity
Roughly: constant mean, variance, and dependence structure over time. Trends and changing seasonality violate strict stationarity. Differencing or seasonal adjustment are tools, not automatic truth.

### 5.7.2.3 Unit-root tests
ADF and related tests have **low power** on short climate records. Do not treat a borderline p-value as proof of a unit root or of “no trend.” Prefer physical and visual assessment alongside formal tests.

**Practice A1.** Compute lag-1 sample autocorrelation for a short synthetic AR(1) series and for white noise; compare ACF plots conceptually.

## 5.7.3 Trend estimation

### 5.7.3.1 OLS trend + HAC / Newey–West SE
Ordinary least squares on time can estimate a slope, but classical SEs assume uncorrelated residuals. Heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors widen intervals when residuals are serially correlated.

### 5.7.3.2 Sen’s slope and Mann–Kendall
Non-parametric slope (Sen) and trend tests (Mann–Kendall) are robust to outliers. Use **autocorrelation-aware variants** (e.g. pre-whitening, block bootstrap) when lag-1 correlation is high.

### 5.7.3.3 What a trend is not
- A 10-year ERA5 area-mean rainfall change is not a flood-causation proof.  
- A reanalysis trend is a statement about the **product** as well as the climate.  
- Short records cannot establish multi-decadal climate-change attribution alone (Unit 2.6).

**Worked sketch (illustrative)**
```python
import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm

# y: annual mean anomaly series; t: year index 0..n-1
t = np.arange(len(y))
X = sm.add_constant(t)
ols = sm.OLS(y, X).fit(cov_type="HAC", cov_kwds={"maxlags": 3})
print(ols.params[1], ols.bse[1])  # slope per year and HAC SE
```
*Requires data; run locally. Choose maxlags with care and report sensitivity.*

## 5.7.4 Spectral analysis and wavelets (overview)

Periodograms highlight dominant periodicities; wavelets localise frequency content in time. Use for exploration and hypothesis generation. Confirm with physical mechanism and multiple independent series—do not “discover” a cycle in every noisy record.

## 5.7.5 Forecasting and evaluation

### 5.7.5.1 Baselines that must be beaten
- **Persistence:** tomorrow ≈ today (or this year ≈ last year).  
- **Climatology:** forecast the seasonal mean.  
- **Seasonal naïve:** this month’s value from last year.

Many ML forecasts fail these baselines after honest evaluation.

### 5.7.5.2 ARIMA / ETS family
Box–Jenkins workflow at a practical level (`statsmodels`, R `fable`). Identify, estimate, diagnose residual ACF, forecast.

### 5.7.5.3 Rolling-origin evaluation
Expanding or sliding windows; never shuffle time randomly for forecasting claims. Report metrics by lead time.

### 5.7.5.4 Changepoints
Structural breaks need pre-specified candidates or penalised search with multiplicity caution. Concurrent events (instrument changes, station moves) are confounders for “climate” breaks.

## 5.7.6 Pitfalls and disciplined language

| Pitfall | Response |
|---------|----------|
| Spurious regression of independent random walks | Difference, or use cointegration ideas carefully |
| Co-trend of \(X\) and \(Y\) ⇒ causal link | Needs Unit 5.10 design |
| UTC vs local day extremes | Fix clocks in Unit 5.2 first |
| “Climate change trend” from 8 years | Limit language to period and product |

**Granger-style predictability** is about incremental forecast skill, **not** physical mechanism by itself.
