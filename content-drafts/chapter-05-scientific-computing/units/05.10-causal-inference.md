# Unit 5.10 — Causal inference for observational environmental data · Support

**Tag:** Support; **exit competency** for causal design  
**Outcome:** Specify a causal estimand; draw a simple DAG; choose a design matched to the data opportunity; state assumptions, diagnostics, sensitivity, and falsifiers; use disciplined language.

**Prerequisites:** 5.4, 5.6; helpful: 5.7.  
**Chapter exit competency:** Define a causal question, identify design and assumptions, state what evidence could weaken or falsify the explanation.

### Learning outcomes (L3)
1. Write a causal estimand in potential-outcomes language (who, treatment, outcome, time).
2. Draw a small DAG distinguishing confounders, mediators, and colliders.
3. Select a design (matching/weighting, DiD, synthetic control, RDD, IV, ITS) that fits the actual assignment process—not the other way around.
4. List identifying assumptions and primary diagnostics for the chosen design.
5. Climb the claim ladder only as far as the design supports.

---

## 5.10.1 Potential outcomes and estimands

For each unit \(i\), imagine potential outcomes under treatment \(Y_i(1)\) and control \(Y_i(0)\). Only one is observed. Causal effects are contrasts such as \(Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\).

**Average treatment effect (ATE):** \(\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)]\).  
**ATT:** average effect on the treated.  

Define clearly: treatment (e.g. tree-planting programme, irrigation ban), outcome (e.g. summer mean LST, not “heat risk” as an undefined composite), population, and period.

---

## 5.10.2 DAGs: confounders, mediators, colliders

Directed acyclic graphs encode qualitative causal assumptions.

- **Confounder:** common cause of treatment and outcome—adjust for it (when measured).  
- **Mediator:** lies on a pathway from treatment to outcome—adjusting blocks that pathway (post-treatment bias if you want the total effect).  
- **Collider:** common effect of two variables—conditioning on it can open a biasing path.

**Environmental example.** Urban tree planting (\(T\)) and summer LST (\(Y\)). Building density and socioeconomic status may confound. Soil moisture may mediate. Pixel-level NDVI regressions rarely isolate the effect of a planting *policy*.

---

## 5.10.3 Core threats to identification

| Threat | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Confounding | Common causes of \(T\) and \(Y\) |
| Selection bias | Conditioning on colliders or selected samples |
| Measurement error | Error in \(T\) or \(Y\) biases effects |
| Interference | One unit’s treatment affects another’s outcome (spatial spillover of cooling, upwind emissions) |
| Positivity | No overlap in covariates between treated and control |

**SUTVA-style assumptions** (no interference, consistent treatment version) are often strained in spatial environmental problems—define exposure carefully (e.g. neighbourhood canopy fraction vs individual tree).

## 5.10.4 Design toolkit

Choose the design that matches how treatment was assigned.

### 5.10.4.1 Matching and propensity weighting
Balance observed covariates between treated and control. Check standardised mean differences. **Does not fix unmeasured confounding.**

### 5.10.4.2 Difference-in-differences (DiD)
Compares change over time in treated vs control groups. Key assumption: **parallel trends** in the absence of treatment. Event-study plots help diagnose pre-trends.

### 5.10.4.3 Synthetic control
Builds a weighted donor pool to match a treated region’s pre-period trajectory. Useful for single treated units (one city, one province). Report weights and placebo tests.

### 5.10.4.4 Regression discontinuity (RDD)
Where a threshold assigns treatment (policy eligibility cutoff). Effects are local to the cutoff. Validate density and covariate smoothness at the cutpoint.

### 5.10.4.5 Instrumental variables (IV)
Needs a source of quasi-random variation that affects outcome **only** through treatment (exclusion restriction). Hard to defend in many environmental settings—state assumptions brutally or avoid.

### 5.10.4.6 Interrupted time series (ITS)
Pre/post comparison with careful seasonality, autocorrelation, and concurrent events. Stronger with a control series (becomes like DiD).

---

## 5.10.5 Spatial confounding and interference

Unmeasured spatial fields (omitted urban form, regional climate) can correlate with both treatment and outcome. Adding spatial smooths or coordinates is a partial, assumption-laden fix. Interference (one park cooling adjacent blocks) means unit-level ATT may not equal a scaled city-wide policy effect.

---

## 5.10.6 Sensitivity analysis and language

Ask: how strong would unmeasured confounding have to be to explain away the estimated effect? (Rosenbaum-style bounds, e-values—use at a conceptual level even if you do not implement full machinery.)

**Claim ladder**
1. Descriptive pattern  
2. Adjusted association  
3. Causal effect **under stated assumptions**  
4. Policy recommendation  

Climb only with design support. “NDVI is significantly negative in an LST regression” stops at (2).

---

## Failure modes
- Causal verbs from cross-sectional pixel regressions.  
- DiD without parallel-trends discussion.  
- IV with a story but no exclusion argument.  
- Ignoring spillovers in spatial treatments.  
- Policy advice from associational EO studies.

## Exercises

**Level A**  
1. Write ATE in potential-outcomes notation for “canal lining reduces waterlogging depth.”  
2. In a DAG with \(T\to M\to Y\) and \(U\to T, U\to Y\), which variables are confounder vs mediator?  
3. Why is positivity a problem if only wealthy neighbourhoods receive tree planting?

**Level B**  
4. For Multan heat and vegetation, write a **non-causal** analysis plan and a **causal** plan that would require different data (e.g. staged planting programme). Contrast estimands.  
5. Sketch parallel trends for a hypothetical cool-roof policy using two cities; list two events that would violate the assumption.

**Level C**  
6. Causal design brief (≤3 pages): question, estimand, DAG, design, assumptions, diagnostics, sensitivity, falsifiers, and data you do *not* have.

### Solution notes (A)
1. \(\mathrm{ATE}=\mathbb{E}[Y(\mathrm{lined})-Y(\mathrm{unlined})]\) for specified units/period.  
2. \(U\) confounder; \(M\) mediator.  
3. No comparable controls for poor neighbourhoods → no support for ATT there without strong extrapolation.

## Unit deliverable
**Causal design brief** (≤3 pages): estimand, DAG, proposed design, assumptions, diagnostics plan, sensitivity plan, falsifiers, and explicit data/assumption gaps. No requirement to implement a full quasi-experiment if data do not support one—honest “not identifiable with current data” is acceptable L3 evidence when reasoned carefully.
