## 5.3.1 EDA as scientific method

### 5.3.1.1 Questions before plots
Useful questions for environmental tables:
- What is the observational unit and support (point, pixel, district)?
- What fraction is missing, and is missingness patterned in space or season?
- What are plausible ranges? Any unit or scale-factor disasters?
- How strong is temporal or spatial dependence?
- Which relationships look linear, monotone, threshold-like, or null?

### 5.3.1.2 Summaries with humility
Mean, median, IQR, quantiles, and **counts of valid observations**. For skewed precipitation, show quantiles or a careful histogram—not only the mean.

### 5.3.1.3 Split-apply-plot
Stratify by season, land-cover class, or sensor when pooling mixes mechanisms (monsoon vs winter rain; urban vs cropland LST).

## 5.3.2 Distributional and relational graphics

### 5.3.2.1 Univariate
Histograms (try more than one bin width), ECDFs (no binning artefact), box/violin plots. Boxplots can hide multimodality—pair with a violin or rug when needed.

### 5.3.2.2 Scatter and smooths
Overplotting: alpha, hexbin, or stated subsampling. A LOESS smooth is exploratory, not a causal curve. Residual-vs-fitted plots after any preliminary fit belong in EDA as well as in modelling (Unit 5.6).

### 5.3.2.3 Correlation displays
Correlation heatmaps are starting points. Nearby pixels inflate pairwise correlation—interpret with dependence in mind.

## 5.3.3 Time and climate graphics

### 5.3.3.1 Series and anomalies
Plot anomalies relative to a **stated** baseline (e.g. 1991–2020). Put baseline and season rule in the caption.

### 5.3.3.2 Seasonal cycles
Day-of-year or month climatologies; small multiples by decade when relevant.

### 5.3.3.3 Dependence diagnostics
ACF/PACF and lag plots before significance tests (Unit 5.7).

### 5.3.3.4 Specialty forms
Warming stripes, Taylor diagrams, wind roses—use when they answer a real question; avoid decorative novelty.

---

## 5.3.4 Maps as analytical graphics

### 5.3.4.1 Required map metadata
Variable, units, date or period, sensor/product, resolution, CRS (or equal-area note), colour limits, missing-data colour.

### 5.3.4.2 Colour and classification
Sequential vs diverging palettes; colour-blind safe schemes; avoid rainbow for continuous scalars. Quantile vs equal-interval vs manual breaks change the story—justify the choice.

### 5.3.4.3 Small multiples
Shared colour scales across panels beat animation-only exploration in written reports.

### 5.3.4.4 Error and residual maps
Map prediction errors and residual structure; unmapped residuals hide failure (Units 5.6, 3.9).

## 5.3.5 Uncertainty and communication design

Error bars, confidence bands, ensemble spaghetti, explicit “no data” regions. Do not use a smooth surface to imply precision the sample lacks. Truncated axes that manufacture drama are a failure mode. Interactive maps help exploration; static figures with fixed provenance help archives and review.

**Worked example — LST vs NDVI (association only)**
```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5, 4))
ax.scatter(df["ndvi"], df["lst_c"], s=2, alpha=0.15, c="0.2")
ax.set_xlabel("NDVI (dimensionless)")
ax.set_ylabel("LST (°C)")
ax.set_title("Multan scene — association only; pixels not independent")
```
*Illustrative — requires data. Caption must refuse causal cooling claims.*

## Failure modes
Rainbow LST scales; district means without \(n\) or variance; “validation” scatter without residuals/holdout; seasonal plots mixing UTC and local days; captions that overclaim.
