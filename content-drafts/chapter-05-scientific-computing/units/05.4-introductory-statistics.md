## 5.4.1 Probability, populations, and sampling

The “population” may be all possible summer days or all districts. **Support** matters: 100 pixels in one neighbourhood do not represent a province.

Sampling designs: simple random, stratified, systematic, clustered. Monitoring networks are often convenience samples—inference needs explicit assumptions.

**Sampling distribution:** distribution of a statistic across repeated samples. Standard error measures precision of the statistic, not spread of raw data.

---

## 5.4.2 Estimation and confidence intervals

Point estimates: mean, median, proportion, slope—each targets a different feature.

**Frequentist CI.** A 95% CI procedure traps the true parameter in 95% of repeated samples *under the model*. It is **not** \(P(\theta\in\text{observed interval})\).

For approximately normal sampling distributions:
\[\bar{x}\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\]
Replace \(n\) with effective sample size when observations are dependent.

**Bootstrap.** Resample (block bootstrap for series) when analytic SE is hard. Record algorithm and seed.

**Worked example.** \(n=30\) approximately independent monthly anomalies, \(\bar{x}=0.8\)°C, \(s=1.2\)°C:
\[\mathrm{SE}\approx0.22,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}\approx[0.37,\ 1.23].\]
If these were 30 consecutive winter *days*, independence is implausible—widen uncertainty (Unit 5.7).

## 5.4.3 Hypothesis tests without theatre

\(H_0\) vs \(H_1\); Type I/II errors. p-value = probability of data as extreme as observed **if \(H_0\) and the model are true**. Small p ≠ large effect; large p ≠ proof of \(H_0\).

Common tests (t, Wilcoxon, χ²) carry assumptions. Classical t-tests on means of consecutive climate days are usually invalid without dependence adjustments.

**Multiple testing.** Many pixels or stations inflate false positives—pre-specify primary tests or control FDR (Unit 5.8 field significance).

## 5.4.4 Correlation and simple regression

Pearson (linear) vs Spearman (rank). Correlation is symmetric and non-causal.

**Simple linear regression**
\[Y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1 X_i+\varepsilon_i\]
Classical assumptions: linearity in parameters, independent errors, constant variance, Gaussian errors for exact small-sample inference. Environmental data often break independence.

\(\beta_1\): expected change in \(Y\) per unit \(X\) **under the model**—units are units of \(Y\) per unit of \(X\).

**Diagnostics:** residuals vs fitted; QQ plot; residuals vs time or space. Patterned residuals invalidate default SEs.

**Association ≠ causation.** Vegetation and LST correlate for many reasons (moisture, irrigation, season, materials). Causal claims need Unit 5.10 designs.

import statsmodels.api as sm
# Illustrative
X = sm.add_constant(df["ndvi"].values)

## 5.4.5 Effect sizes, power, non-parametrics

Report \(\hat\beta\) or mean differences alongside p-values. A 0.05°C effect can be “significant” in a huge sample and irrelevant for heat risk.

Power rises with effect size and \(n\), falls with noise, stricter α, and dependence. Use power thinking when designing campaigns.

Rank tests and bootstrap when Gaussian assumptions fail—still not assumption-free (independence often remains).
