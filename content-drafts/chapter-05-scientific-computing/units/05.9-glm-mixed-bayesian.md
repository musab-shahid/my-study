## 5.9.1 Generalised linear models

### 5.9.1.1 Why leave ordinary least squares
OLS assumes roughly continuous, constant-variance, unbounded errors. Binary exceedance, counts of rain days, and skewed positive concentrations need other likelihoods.

### 5.9.1.2 Family and link
A GLM specifies a distribution for \(Y\mid\mathbf{x}\) and a link \(g(\mu)=\mathbf{x}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}\) connecting the mean \(\mu=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\mathbf{x}]\) to predictors.

| Response | Common family | Typical link |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| Binary (0/1) | Bernoulli / binomial | logit |
| Counts | Poisson or negative binomial | log |
| Positive continuous | Gamma | log |
| Unbounded continuous | Normal (OLS as GLM) | identity |

**Overdispersion:** Poisson assumes variance = mean. If counts are more variable, prefer negative binomial or quasi-likelihood and report it.

### 5.9.1.3 Interpretation
On the link scale, coefficients are additive; on the mean scale they are not. For logit models, report odds ratios *and* predicted probabilities at meaningful covariate values—not only coefficient tables.

### 5.9.1.4 Diagnostics
Deviance or Pearson residuals; influence measures; for probability models, calibration (reliability) plots. Residual dependence in space/time still matters—GLMs do not remove the need for blocked validation (Unit 5.6).

### 5.9.1.5 Worked example — synthetic heat exceedance (executed)

**Data are synthetic.** File: `fixtures/synthetic_heat_exceedance.csv` (n = 200). They are **not** Multan, Landsat, or ERA5 observations. Generator seed: `numpy.random.default_rng(20261021)`. Binary outcome `exceed` was drawn from a Bernoulli logit with known coefficients (intercept −1.2, `lst_anom_c` +0.55, `ndvi` −2.0, `built_frac` +1.1). Fitted estimates below differ from those truths because of sampling variability.

| Column | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `lst_anom_c` | Synthetic local temperature anomaly (°C) |
| `ndvi` | Synthetic greenness-like index |
| `built_frac` | Synthetic built-up fraction in [0, 1] |
| `exceed` | Binary 1 = “exceedance” under the synthetic DGP |

**Why logistic?** The outcome is binary. OLS can produce fitted values outside [0, 1] and assumes constant variance; a Bernoulli/binomial likelihood with a logit link maps a linear predictor to probabilities in (0, 1).

**Model**
\[
\log\frac{\pi_i}{1-\pi_i}
= \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{lst\_anom\_c}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{ndvi}_i + \beta_3\,\mathrm{built\_frac}_i,
\qquad
Y_i\mid\mathbf{x}_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi_i).
\]

```python
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

df = pd.read_csv("fixtures/synthetic_heat_exceedance.csv")
model = smf.glm(
    "exceed ~ lst_anom_c + ndvi + built_frac",
    data=df,
    family=sm.families.Binomial(),
).fit()
print(model.summary())
```

**Executed fit** (statsmodels 0.14.x, IRLS, 5 iterations; no complete separation — fitted probabilities ranged about 0.009–0.85):

| Term | Coefficient | Std. error | z | P>\|z\| | Odds ratio \(e^{\hat\beta}\) |
|------|-------------|------------|---|--------|------------------------------|
| Intercept | −0.586 | 0.541 | −1.08 | 0.279 | 0.557 |
| `lst_anom_c` | 0.440 | 0.080 | 5.49 | <0.001 | 1.553 |
| `ndvi` | −2.365 | 1.200 | −1.97 | 0.049 | 0.094 |
| `built_frac` | 0.470 | 0.808 | 0.58 | 0.561 | 1.600 |

**Interpretation (log-odds):** holding other predictors fixed, a +1 °C increase in `lst_anom_c` is associated with a 0.440 increase in log-odds of exceedance. **Odds ratio:** \(e^{0.440}\approx 1.55\), i.e. about 55% higher odds per °C under the model — **not** a causal effect of temperature on real heat risk, and not transferable to Multan without new data and design.

**Predicted probabilities** (inverse logit of the linear predictor), independently recomputed from the coefficients above:

| Setting | `lst_anom_c` | `ndvi` | `built_frac` | \(\hat\pi\) |
|---------|--------------|--------|--------------|-------------|
| A cooler / greener / less built | −1.0 | 0.50 | 0.20 | **0.108** |
| B warmer / less green / more built | 2.0 | 0.15 | 0.70 | **0.567** |

**Calibration-style check (descriptive only):** quartile bins of \(\hat\pi\) vs observed exceedance rates on the same n = 200 rows — Q1 obs ≈ 0.08 vs mean \(\hat\pi\) ≈ 0.07; Q4 obs ≈ 0.62 vs mean \(\hat\pi\) ≈ 0.61. Agreement is encouraging for this **in-sample synthetic** fit; it does **not** establish out-of-sample predictive validity or real-world calibration. Spatial/temporal dependence is absent by construction of the fixture (Unit 5.6 still applies to real environmental tables).

**Limitations**
- Synthetic DGP ≠ physical heat process; coefficients are not environmental facts.
- Association only; no Unit 5.10 design.
- Independent rows by construction — real LST/NDVI fields are dependent.
- `built_frac` SE is large; the estimate is consistent with noise at this sample size.
- Re-run: `python content-drafts/chapter-05-scientific-computing/fixtures/smoke_check.py` (includes `check_logistic_glm`).

### 5.9.1.5 GAMs (connection)
Generalised additive models replace some linear terms with smooth functions (e.g. nonlinear temperature response). Penalisation limits overfitting. Use when residual plots show clear nonlinearity and sample size supports smooth estimation.

## 5.9.2 Mixed-effects and hierarchical models

### 5.9.2.1 Why hierarchical structure
Stations within districts; pixels within Landsat scenes; years within decades. Complete pooling (ignore groups) understates uncertainty; no pooling (separate model per group) is unstable for small groups. **Partial pooling** via random effects compromises sensibly.

### 5.9.2.2 Random intercept model
\[
y_{ij} = \beta_0 + u_j + \beta_1 x_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ij},
\qquad u_j\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_u^2)
\]
Here \(j\) indexes group (e.g. station), \(i\) observation within group. \(\sigma_u\) is between-group SD.

Random slopes allow the effect of \(x\) to vary by group—use when theory and data support heterogeneity; they need more data.

### 5.9.2.3 Software
Python: `statsmodels` mixed linear models; `pymer4` bridge to R. R: `lme4` remains a standard. Report package and version.

### 5.9.2.4 Survey weights (overview)
Unequal-probability samples need design weights for unbiased descriptive estimates and appropriate SEs—coordinate with Subject 8 when analysing surveys.

## 5.9.3 Bayesian reasoning and computation

### 5.9.3.1 Prior, likelihood, posterior
Bayes updates prior information with the likelihood of the data to produce a posterior distribution for parameters. **Priors are part of the model**—state them. Weakly informative priors still influence small samples.

### 5.9.3.2 Credible intervals
A 95% credible interval is a posterior probability statement about the parameter (given model and prior). That is philosophically different from a frequentist confidence interval’s coverage property. Do not use the words interchangeably.

### 5.9.3.3 Computation (conceptual)
Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) draws approximate samples from the posterior (Stan, PyMC, etc.). Check convergence: R-hat near 1, adequate effective sample size, no divergent transitions for HMC. Start from tested tutorials before custom hierarchical models.

### 5.9.3.4 Posterior predictive checks
Simulate replicated datasets from the posterior predictive distribution; compare to observed data (distributions, extremes, spatial patterns). Systematic mismatch means the model is inadequate for those features—even if parameters look “significant.”

**Recommended learning path:** McElreath, *Statistical Rethinking*, for workflow intuition; Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, for depth.

## Failure modes
- Logistic regression reported only as coefficients without predicted probabilities.  
- Poisson fit despite clear overdispersion.  
- Mixed model without stating grouping factor or checking residual intra-group correlation.  
- Bayesian results without stating priors or diagnostics.  
- Treating GLM fitted values as causal effects.
