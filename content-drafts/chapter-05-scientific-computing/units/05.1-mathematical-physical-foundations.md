## 5.1.1 Quantities, units, and dimensional analysis

### 5.1.1.1 Why units fail quietly
Every number should answer: quantity, unit, spatial support, time period.

| Quantity | SI unit | Notes |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Temperature | K | Use K in radiation laws |
| Power / flux | W m⁻² | Energy balance |
| Precipitation | m or mm | ERA5 often **metres** of accumulation |

**Conversion:** \(T_K = T_{^\circ C}+273.15\). Depth vs rate: mm vs mm day⁻¹ are different.

### 5.1.1.2 Dimensional homogeneity
Both sides of an equation need the same dimensions. \(P=E+R+\Delta S\) requires consistent depth (or volume/area) over the same period.

**Worked example.** Hourly ERA5 precipitation in metres → multiply by 1000 for mm; sum hours on the same **PKT local** day (UTC+5) for mm day⁻¹. UTC-day aggregation biases local extremes.

### 5.1.1.3 Precision and Fermi checks
Do not report eight decimal places from floats. Is a 40 K Multan urban–rural LST difference plausible, or was a scale factor applied twice?

**Practice A1.** Decode integer ST-like storage: \(T_K=\mathrm{raw}\times\mathrm{scale}+\mathrm{offset}\) (verify current USGS factors before operational use). Convert to °C. What fails if offset is omitted?

## 5.1.2 Calculus and rates of change

### 5.1.2.1 Derivative and finite differences
\[\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x}=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\]
Centred difference: \(f'(x)\approx[f(x+\Delta x)-f(x-\Delta x)]/(2\Delta x)\). Daily \(\Delta t=1\) estimates an average rate over the day—state units (°C day⁻¹ vs °C decade⁻¹).

### 5.1.2.2 Partials and integrals
Field \(T(x,y,t)\): partials hold other variables fixed; \(\nabla T\) is steepest ascent in the plane. Integrals accumulate rates to totals; missing days matter.

**Practice A2.** \(T=[30,32,31,33]\) °C daily. Centred differences at interior points; units.

## 5.1.3 Linear algebra for data

### 5.1.3.1 Matrices as tables
\(\mathbf{X}_{n\times p}\): rows = samples, columns = variables. Dot product and \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\) as linear maps.

### 5.1.3.2 Least squares as projection
OLS minimises \(\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|^2\); fitted values project \(\mathbf{y}\) onto the column space of \(\mathbf{X}\).

### 5.1.3.3 Eigenvalues and SVD
\(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}\). PCA uses covariance eigenstructure or SVD of centred data. Prefer `numpy.linalg.eigh` / `svd`.

**Worked example.** \(\mathbf{S}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.8\\0.8&1\end{pmatrix}\) → \(\lambda\approx1.8,0.2\); PC1 along shared variability; 90% variance in PC1.

```python
import numpy as np
S = np.array([[1.0, 0.8], [0.8, 1.0]])
evals, evecs = np.linalg.eigh(S)
order = np.argsort(evals)[::-1]
print(evals[order])  # ~ [1.8 0.2]
```
*Illustrative — run locally.*

An eigenvalue describes **your** matrix (domain, season, weights), not a physical law (Unit 5.8).

**Practice A3.** Verify \(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}_1=\lambda_1\mathbf{v}_1\) numerically.

## 5.1.4 ODEs and numerical methods

Stock–flux: \(\mathrm{d}S/\mathrm{d}t=F_{in}-F_{out}(S)\). Lake example: \(\mathrm{d}M/\mathrm{d}t=L-(k+Q/V)M\); steady state \(M=L/(k+Q/V)\).

Euler: \(S_{n+1}=S_n+\Delta t\,f(t_n,S_n)\). Can be unstable; use `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` in practice.

**Practice A4.** \(\mathrm{d}C/\mathrm{d}t=-0.5C\), \(C(0)=10\); two Euler steps \(\Delta t=1\) vs exact \(10e^{-0.5t}\).

## 5.1.5 Environmental physics

**Water balance:** include irrigation/groundwater in Indus plain thinking.  
**Energy balance:** \(R_n=H+LE+G\) (state sign convention; W m⁻²).  
**Stefan–Boltzmann:** \(E=\varepsilon\sigma T^4\), \(\sigma=5.670374419\times10^{-8}\), \(T\) in kelvin.

**Example.** Night: \(R_n=-40\), \(G=-15\), \(LE\approx0\) → \(H=-25\) W m⁻² under stated signs. LST ≠ 2 m air temperature.

## 5.1.6 Probability foundations

Bayes: \(P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B)\).  
Working distributions: Normal, Poisson, Gamma, empirical.  
SE of mean \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\) assumes i.i.d.; autocorrelation shrinks effective \(n\) (Units 5.6–5.7).

**Practice A5.** \(P(\mathrm{alarm}|\mathrm{poll})=0.9\), \(P(\mathrm{alarm}|\mathrm{clean})=0.1\), prior 0.05. Find \(P(\mathrm{poll}|\mathrm{alarm})\).  
**Solution:** \(P(B)=0.14\); posterior \(\approx0.32\).
